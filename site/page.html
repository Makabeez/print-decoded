One sentence to remember

Pacifica describes Print simply: pick a target, lock a deposit, get paid every cycle, and fill only if the market is past your target at the end of the cycle. Now look at what you're actually promising.

If the checkpoint mark is below your Print Long target, you buy at the target anyway. You start the position already down by the gap, times your leverage. If it's above, nothing happens and you keep the premium. That payoff has a name.

A Print Long is a put you sold, struck at your target, expiring at the checkpoint. A Print Short is a call you sold.

The Print pool is the buyer. The "yield" is the option premium. Leverage is just how many options you sold. And because your loss stops at your deposit plus premium, it's really a put spread: the cap is a second put you're long, struck at your liquidation price.

Profit or loss at the checkpoint, as % of your deposit, against where the mark price finishes. Your Print and a plain short option with the same strike and premium are the same line until the liquidation cap.

Why half your touches vanish

A limit order fills the moment the market touches your price. A Print only looks once, at the checkpoint. How much does that cost in fills? I ran every 24-hour window on Pacifica's hourly mark candles, starting at every hour, for every Print market, and on BTC for the 7-day and 30-day cycles too.

Each dot is one product, side and target distance. Across the axis: how often a limit order at that price would have filled within the cycle. Up: how often a Print filled at the checkpoint. The dots sit on the "half" line, not the "same" line.

That's the reflection principle from probability. If a price wanders without a trend, for every path that touches your target and ends beyond it, there's a mirror-image path that touches it and comes back. So the chance of ending past a level is about half the chance of touching it. Pacifica's data lands almost exactly on it: median fill-to-touch ratio 0.50.

The practical reading: the premium is what the pool pays you to give up the bounces. Those are the dips a limit order would have caught and that recovered by the checkpoint, like the one above. If you'd have been happy to own those dips, price that in before calling the yield free.

Print leverage isn't the leverage you think

The docs give the Print liquidation price as roughly target ÷ leverage beyond your target. That's the point where your deposit plus premium is gone. But at the checkpoint your order turns into an ordinary perp position, and ordinary perps get liquidated earlier: when equity falls below maintenance margin, which on Pacifica is half the initial margin at the market's max leverage.

With nothing but the Print's own margin behind it, the line you really face is:

Long:  L = (K − margin ÷ size) ÷ (1 − 1/(2·maxLev))
Short: L = (K + margin ÷ size) ÷ (1 + 1/(2·maxLev))

What five real premiums were worth

Print quotes come from a signed request, so I can't scrape them. But five real orders sit in public Print history (they're the test fixtures of the open-source perp-cli integration). For each, I solved for the volatility that makes a Black–Scholes price of the capped payoff equal the premium actually paid, then compared it with what the market had realized.

On BTC, the premium was close to fair: 33% implied against 33–35% realized. On ZEC and HYPE, it implied 60–91% while 7-day realized volatility was 92–110%. Five orders prove nothing about the pricing engine, and BS ignores fat tails in both directions. The point is the habit: check the implied vol of your quote against recent realized vol before you lock a month of deposit. The planner above does it for you.

Three ways traders actually use it

Get paid to buy the dip you wanted

If you'd place the limit order anyway, Print is the better-paid version for anyone who's fine missing intraday wicks. Set the target where you'd genuinely want the position, and size leverage so the real liquidation line is far beyond it.

The Print strangle

A Print Long below and a Print Short above on the same market: two premiums, and at most one side can fill per checkpoint. At 3% on BTC, neither side filled in of 24-hour cycles; on HYPE, . That's a short strangle, so it loses on big moves either way.

Get paid to take profit

Holding a long? A Print Short above it works like a covered call: a fill delivers a short position into your main account at your target, offsetting the long there. Until then you collect premium. Try the netting with a $10 order first.

Rules of thumb

  1. Treat the yield as option premium. It's big when the market is moving because that's when your target is most likely to be crossed.
  2. Expect about half as many fills as a limit order at the same price. The missing half are the bounces.
  3. On a fill, expect to start under water. You enter at the target even when the market has run past it; the average overshoot on fills is in the planner.
  4. Real liquidation buffer ≈ 1/leverage − 1/(2 × market max leverage). At max Print leverage on a 10× market, that's 5.3%, not 10%.
  5. Longer cycle, lower leverage. A month lets the market overshoot by a lot before the only check that counts.
  6. Cancelling takes effect at the next checkpoint. Pick cycle length for how long you can live with locked capital.

How this was measured

Price data: Pacifica mark-price candles, 1-hour (GET /api/v1/kline/mark), from each market's first candle to 16 Nov 2026; the hero uses 5-minute candles. A cycle starts at every hour; the target is set from the opening mark; "touch" uses the cycle's hourly low or high; "fill" and "liquidation" use the closing mark at the checkpoint, as Print does. Products and limits: GET /api/v1/game/config. Maintenance margin: ½ × 1/maxLeverage per the Pacifica liquidation docs, maxLeverage from GET /api/v1/info. Premiums: five orders from public /game/history data. Fair value: Black–Scholes on the capped put (or call) spread at the volatility shown. Not financial advice; Print can lose your whole deposit. Code and data to rebuild every number: github.com/Makabeez/print-decoded (make reproduce, public API only).
